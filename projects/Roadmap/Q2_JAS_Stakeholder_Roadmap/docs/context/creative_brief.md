# CEO Operating Plan - Creative Brief

**Status:** CANONICAL v1.2
**Audience:** CEO  
**Artifact:** Proposed 12-page JAS Q2 operating plan plus appendix; final medium pending  
**Truth version:** `project_truth.md` v3.3
**Evidence version:** `evidence_register.md` v1.1
**Blueprint status/version:** `03_operating_plan_blueprint.md` v3.1 FROZEN BASELINE; `DEC-01` resolved

This brief governs expression. It protects creative freedom while keeping facts and strategy stable.

## 1. Assignment

CEO should understand:

- JAS has moved from issue-led delivery toward an outcome-led operating model in three months.
- Q2 portfolio is designed around Customer Experience through Order Completion, Cost to Serve, and Trust.
- Platform & Product Foundations make those outcomes measurable and repeatable.
- Q1 created value and exposed weak measurement, recurring operating friction, and delivery-capacity constraints.
- Q2 contains hypotheses and foundational work; unknowns are explicit, not hidden.
- Plan requires clear prioritisation, Analytics commitment, and an execution-support decision.

CEO should leave able to answer:

1. Why these initiatives?
2. Which customer/business outcomes should move?
3. What evidence exists and what remains unknown?
4. Can current team execute this portfolio?
5. What decisions or support are required now?

## 2. Core narrative

```text
Issue-led delivery and production stabilisation
                ↓

                ↓
Outcome-led operating model
                ↓
Deduplicated Q2 portfolio
                ↓
Measurement contract and foundations
                ↓

                ↓
Exact leadership decisions
```

Transformation is progress story, not criticism of prior work.

## 3. Non-negotiable strategy

- Preserve locked outcome tree from `project_truth.md`.
- Keep each of 11 initiatives in exactly one primary home.
- Show secondary effects only as badges or notes.
- Treat Foundations as enabling layer beneath outcomes, not fourth customer outcome.
- Keep Ring AI as cart recovery.
- Keep CSR Voicebot Phase 2 as direct Cost-to-Serve hypothesis.
- Keep TM Wallet and R&R under Trust; Doctor Experience, CRM, and Core Configuration under Foundations.
- Treat Juspay as implementation enabler.
- Treat Discard Intelligence as attribution and lever-discovery capability first.
- Page 11 shows initiative lifecycle across quarter, never engineer swimlanes.
- Page 12 remains provisional and is rewritten after Pages 1-11 are populated.

## 4. Creative freedom

Freeze creates shared comparison baseline, not final design. Independent providers should express same truth differently. Better structure may be proposed, reviewed, and adopted later through explicit change log and user-approved source update.

Agents may:

- Rewrite page titles, headlines, and narrative.
- Propose stronger visual treatment.
- Reorder blocks within a page.
- Condense, combine, or remove weak/repetitive content.
- Recommend page-sequence changes through explicit proposal.
- Challenge whether one page earns its place.
- Create stronger CEO takeaway or decision framing.
- Use different storytelling approaches across independent drafts.

Agents must not:

- Invent or silently change facts, targets, dates, owners, estimates, or status.
- Move or duplicate initiatives without explicit structural proposal.
- Present hypothesis as observed result.
- Turn unknown into estimate.
- Treat source-reported values as independently validated when definitions conflict.
- Turn roadmap into feature catalogue or engineer allocation sheet.
- Turn execution-support ask into personal workload complaint.
- Reopen resolved decisions without new user evidence.

Structural challenge format:

```text
Current element
Proposed change
Reason
CEO benefit
Truth/evidence impact
Pages affected
```

Never alter canonical structure silently.

## 5. Voice

- Assertive, evidence-led, direct.
- Honest about uncertainty.
- Customer/business outcome before technology.
- CEO altitude; minimal product jargon.
- Specific language over generic labels such as `improve experience` or `build intelligence` without mechanism.
- No defensive explanation, inflated transformation language, or invented precision.
- Distinguish `observed`, `source reports`, `working baseline`, `hypothesis`, and `unknown` visibly.

## 6. Visual grammar

- One dominant message and one primary visual per page.
- Maximum three major content zones unless page is portfolio/timeline table.
- Outcomes retain consistent visual identity across deck.
- Foundations remain visually beneath/supporting outcomes.
- Evidence, hypothesis, and unknown states use distinct labels or treatment.
- Main pages show only decision-useful metrics; detailed dimensions move to appendix/Analytics pack.
- Trees show hierarchy; tables show exact mappings; timelines show lifecycle and gates.
- Avoid decorative diagrams, dense schemas, feature inventories, and tiny text.
- Visual must remain understandable without narration.

## 7. Evidence language

Every numeric claim carries evidence-register ID during drafting; presentation can move IDs into notes/appendix later.

| Evidence status | Drafting language |
|---|---|
| FILE-VERIFIED | State directly when consistent; otherwise `source reports`. |
| USER-SUPPLIED | `Working baseline; validation required`. |
| DERIVED | Show formula or cited inputs. |
| HYPOTHESIS | `Hypothesis`, `aspiration`, or `to prove`. |
| UNKNOWN | State data request and decision it unlocks. |
| CONFLICTED | Do not use as CEO claim. |

## 8. Page quality bar

Each page must pass:

- One CEO takeaway.
- Clear role in full story.
- Initiative mapping remains correct.
- No unsupported or denominator-free claim.
- Evidence status visible.
- Visual understandable in five seconds, detail readable in thirty.
- Unknowns linked to decision, not presented as apology.
- No repetition already handled better elsewhere.
- Clear implication: understand, believe, decide, or support.
