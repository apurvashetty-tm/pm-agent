C

Where Price Lock does not act

Boundaries, not levers.
Replace with OG no absorb

Substitute unavailable, original given instead. Higher price is not absorbed — Ops removes the item or takes consent.

New product added no absorb

A product added at fulfilment isn't a variance on a promised line. No price lock applies.

Quantity reduced refund

Fewer units fulfilled → refund for the missing units. Per-unit FSP unchanged; refund is the payment service's job.

Pack-size change absorbed

Different pack size of the same medicine — price difference is absorbed, exactly as today. In scope.

Substitute B→C absorbed

A different generic substitute picked — treated like a batch change and absorbed. In scope.

Doctor / HA / CSR edit re-lock

A change on these calls re-locks the FSP for that item at that stage. The lock is per-item, not a single order-placement value.

01

The one idea

Prices move between order and fulfilment — a different batch, a pack-size swap, a substitute. Today the customer's price can shift; tomorrow it can't.

Today
  • The discount re-applies to whatever batch is picked.
  • So the price floats — protected on a spike, dropped on a dip.
Tomorrow
  • We lock the FSP the customer agreed to.
  • The discount is just Batch MRP − FSP. The price is the fixed point.

The two sides only differ on a lower batch — that gap is the whole decision. The interactive above walks each case (higher batches land the same either way); this write-up covers the parts it can't show.

02

The promise

The quoted price is the promise. Each line's price is its locked FSP; a coupon sits on top (whole order) and the bill shows one blended discount. Tomorrow's invoice lands back on that promised total.

Coupon is on MRP, and independent of the lockFIRST23 is a flat 23% off MRP — not stacked on the base discount. It lowers every FSP; the batch-variance logic doesn't touch it. They compose cleanly.
03

Guardrails & exceptions

⚠ This does not exist today

Today there is no cap and no floor. Cost absorption absorbs any increase (the threshold is switched off) and passes down any decrease — an unusually mispriced batch is eaten silently. Tomorrow adds two guardrails, and a breach of either becomes an exception, never a silent adjustment.

Higher-MRP absorption cap
  • The most Price Lock will absorb before it's flagged.
  • Over it → exception, not silent absorption.
Lower-MRP threshold
  • How far the batch MRP may fall below quoted before it's flagged.
  • The trigger is the threshold; FSP is only the legal floor beneath it.
How Problem Solver gets involved
Guardrail breached→ Post-order flags the line→ Problem Solver→ alternate batch · operational review · customer decision · cancel
Scope & open numberPrice Lock only routes to Problem Solver. Problem Solver's resolution SOP and alternate-batch selection are separate scope. Both threshold values are new product knobs — set deliberately, then confirm with finance.
04

The contract

Today
  • Discount context travels forward and is re-derived against the picked batch.
Tomorrow
  • Two live fields: locked FSP + quoted MRP.
  • Base/coupon/tax = frozen metadata, never recomputed post-order.
  • Re-lock: a Doctor / HA / CSR edit re-locks that item at that stage.
Warehouse edits stay as they are todayAny warehouse-side edit — order edit, bulk update, quantity change — behaves exactly as it does now; Price Lock doesn't change it. But because those edits change what's actually fulfilled, tax and charges must be re-validated after any such edit, before the invoice is finalised.

The invoice

Compare the two documents above. Minimal change: drop Old MRP (the legacy GST-transition column, usually blank), keep one MRP (the batch billed), keep one blended Discount. No separate Price Lock line — that call-out lives in the scenario panel, not on the customer's invoice.

The absorption story moves, it doesn't disappear"We covered ₹X for you" lives on the app's Price Lock page, not the tax invoice. "Selling Price" / FSP is an internal name — not an invoice column.
06

Customer-facing surfaces

Today
  • Higher MRP → "extra cost avoided" delight.
  • Lower MRP → "your bill reduced" delight + refund.
Tomorrow
  • Higher MRP → "protected by Price Lock" stays.
  • Lower MRP → no post-order drop. The pre-order "Your savings" is untouched.
The real fix lives upstreamA cheaper batch should mean the customer was quoted the cheaper price — an MCP / correct-batch-picking job, not an invoice-time refund.
07

Ownership & scope

In scope
  • Lock FSP + carry quoted MRP.
  • Derived discount, the two guardrails, exception flagging.
  • Invoice simplification, batch rows, refund snapshot.
Out of scope
  • Variance correction at source — MCP / picking. The real fix, currently unowned.
  • Returns, pack-size orchestration, coupon/tax redesign — continue as-is.
The dependency to name in the PRDPrice Lock makes upstream pricing accuracy more urgent and more measurable — yet no one owns it today. Needs an explicit owner.
08

The upstream fix — MCP

How the right price gets quoted in the first place.

Price Lock holds the promise; it doesn't shrink the variance. The variance shrinks upstream — by quoting the right price at order time. That's the job of MCP (Most Common Price), and it becomes more important the moment Price Lock stops papering over it.

How MCP picks the price
  • Not picking logic — MCP just returns the MRP of the batch with the most quantity in pickable zones.
  • Pickable = JIT · Urgent · Excess. Bulk is excluded (non-pickable).
BatchPickable qtyMRP
A30₹100
B20₹120
C10₹80
Highest pickable qty = 30 → MCP MRP = ₹100
Then what happens
MCP quotes ₹100→ Picking is MRP-blind today→ Post-order sees the picked MRP
  • If MCP + correct-batch picking are accurate, the quoted price already matches the fulfilled batch — little to nothing to absorb.
  • Every rupee Price Lock absorbs is a rupee MCP or picking got wrong. Absorption becomes the measure of upstream inaccuracy.
Why this closes the loopToday absorption hides the miss; tomorrow it's a capped, counted cost. That visibility is the case for giving MCP / correct-batch picking an explicit owner — the real fix for variance.
